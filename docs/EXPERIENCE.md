# EXPERIENCE — who is on the other side of the screen

## The three people who arrive

**The portal drifter.** Came from a grid of thumbnails on CrazyGames. Has
fifteen seconds of patience and has already closed four other games today. Will
not sign up for anything, will not read anything.
→ *Must be playing before deciding whether to play.*

**The returning player.** Has a run they are proud of, a skin they like, and
about eleven hundred coins. Opened the site to keep going. Everything else on
the page is in their way.
→ *One button, and it says continue.*

**The buyer.** Wants an OP skin. Has been sent to a payment form by other games
and abandoned it. Is deciding, in this order: is this real, is it safe, what
exactly do I get, and can I get help if it goes wrong.
→ *Every one of those questions answered before the price is shown.*

## The rules that follow

### 1. Play before signup. Always.
The landing page's primary action is **Play now**, and it starts the game with
no account. Signing in is offered *after* the first run ends, at the moment it
means something: "that run earned 40 coins — sign in to keep them."

A signup wall in front of a free browser game loses more players than it
converts. The account has to be worth wanting first.

### 2. Never lose someone's progress without warning them.
Anonymous play saves to the browser and that is fragile. So:
- Say so plainly, once, on the death screen: *progress is saved on this device
  only.*
- When they sign in, **merge** what is on the device into the account rather
  than replacing either. Nobody should have to choose between two versions of
  themselves.
- Never silently overwrite. If both sides have progress, keep the higher of
  each and say what happened.

### 3. Money is explained before it is asked for.
Before any price: what it is, whether it is permanent, and that support exists.
Prices in AED with no conversion trickery. No countdown timers on a first
purchase — the daily offer rotates on the date and says so.

### 4. Errors say what to do next.
"Something went wrong" is not an error message. Each one names the thing that
failed and the next action. A failed payment says whether money moved.

### 5. The game is the loud thing; the site is quiet.
The site's job is to get out of the way. Everything on it can be read at a
glance, and nothing animates for its own sake.

## The journeys

### First visit
```
Landing → sees the game running → Play now → plays → dies
   → "40 coins earned. Sign in to keep them." → signs up → Home
```
Failure to guard: signing up and landing on an empty Home with nothing to do.
**Home must show the coins they just earned, in the first paint.**

### Coming back
```
Opens the site → already signed in → Home → Continue
```
Two clicks from URL to playing. If it takes more, the header is wrong.

### Forgot the password
```
Sign in → Forgot password → email → "check your inbox, it may take a minute"
   → link → new password → signed in, on Home
```
Never reveal whether an address has an account — the message is identical
either way. The link expires in an hour and says so.

### Something went wrong
```
Support → FAQ answers it (most do) → or contact form
   → visible confirmation with a reference → email arrives
```
The confirmation must appear on the page. "Sent" that only exists as an email
they have not received yet reads as failure.

## What we watch, and what we do not

**Watch:** how many who land actually start playing; how many who play sign up;
where a first run ends; how far a session gets before it is closed; how many
buyers finish a purchase once started.

**Do not watch:** anything requiring a consent banner we would then have to
apologise for. No third-party tracking pixels. The numbers above can all be
counted server-side against an account or an anonymous session id, and that is
enough to run the business.

## Content voice

Plain, short, and it never pretends. "Real-money purchases are not built yet"
is what the shop says today, because that is true and a coming-soon badge would
not be. The same voice on the site: say what is, say what is not.

## Accessibility as experience, not compliance

- The game needs a mouse and keyboard, or a touchscreen. It cannot be played
  by keyboard alone and pretending otherwise helps nobody — so **every part of
  the site around it can be**, and the shop, the account and support are fully
  operable without ever entering the canvas.
- Text is resizable to 200% without anything overlapping.
- The tablet layout is not the desktop layout shrunk; the controls were
  designed for it separately, and so are the pages.
